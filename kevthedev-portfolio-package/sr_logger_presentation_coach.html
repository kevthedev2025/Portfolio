The problem — make them feel it
~2 min
Right now, every SR that comes into ICT COM Support mailbox gets manually processed — the consultant reads the email, finds the SR number in the subject line, opens the BOM PDF, and types 14 fields into the BPI Workbook on SharePoint. By hand.
That's 15 minutes per SR. On a busy day — up to 15 SRs. That's three and a half hours of a skilled consultant's day, gone.
And there's no duplicate check, no scheduling — if a reply email comes in, it could get logged twice. We're one mis-click away from a corrupted SR_Master.
💡 Pause after the "three and a half hours" line. Let it sink in. Don't rush to the solution yet.
2
The solution — what it does, simply
~2 min
SR Logger is a Power Automate flow that runs every 15 minutes. It scans the mailbox, ignores any replies or forwards, finds the new SRs, opens the BOM attachment, extracts all 14 fields using AI Builder, and writes them directly into SR_Master — automatically.
No consultant touches it. By the time they get to work in the morning, the overnight SRs are already logged.
If anything goes wrong — wrong format, confidence too low — it sends an alert. Nothing falls through silently.
💡 Point to the flow diagram when you describe the steps. Walk left to right — START, check the subject, branch the decision, extract, write, loop. Don't read every node, just narrate the journey.
3
The numbers — make the ROI stick
~1 min
Conservative estimate: 30 consultant hours freed per month. Optimistic: 77. Either way, that's time that goes straight back into the backlog — the work that actually needs human judgment.
This is MVP 1. It only does logging. Once it's stable, MVP 2 adds pre-screening and allocation — but I'm not asking for that today. Just logging, just this system.
💡 Say "I'm not asking for MVP 2 today" — it shows discipline and makes the ask feel small and safe.
4
The ask — be specific
~1 min
Everything I need exists in our current O365 stack — Power Automate, AI Builder, SharePoint. No new tools, no new spend.
The one thing blocking me is a DLP policy that restricts the Power Automate connector to the shared mailbox. I need that allowlisted. That's the decision I'm asking Donovan to support today.
Once that's done, I have a 6-week delivery plan — environment setup, flow build, AI Builder training, UAT, go-live.
💡 Name Donovan directly and look at him when you say it. You're not asking the room — you're asking one person for one specific thing.
5
The close — confidence, not begging
~30 sec
This is low risk, high return, and it uses tools we already pay for. I've done the feasibility work, I've mapped the fields, I've built the flow diagram. I'm ready to build it — I just need the DLP unblocked.
Happy to take any questions.

What if AI Builder reads the BOM wrong? Hard
Don't panic — you've thought of this.

"AI Builder gives us a confidence score on every extraction. I'll set a threshold — anything below that score triggers an alert to the consultant to manually check that SR. So worst case, we're back to manual for that one SR. Nothing falls through silently, and we never write bad data to SR_Master automatically."
What happens to the fails — SRs that don't pass pre-screening? Hard
This is intentionally out of scope — own it confidently.

"MVP 1 only handles SRs that have already passed pre-screening — same as today. The consultant still does pre-screening manually. I've scoped it this way deliberately so we can deliver value fast and prove the concept before expanding. Pre-screening is MVP 2."
Why can't you just use a macro or a script instead? Hard
Acknowledge it, then explain why Power Automate is the right call here.

"A script could do the mailbox scanning, but it can't read a PDF and extract structured fields without significant custom development — and it would need a machine to be running continuously. Power Automate with AI Builder is purpose-built for exactly this pattern, it's hosted in our O365 tenant, it's auditable, and it's maintainable by the team without a developer."
How long will it take to build? Medium
"Six weeks from DLP approval to go-live. Two weeks setup, two weeks flow build and AI Builder training, two weeks UAT and parallel-run against the manual process. Ismut signs off at the end."
What's the cost? Medium
"No additional spend. Power Automate and AI Builder are already part of our O365 licensing. The only cost is my time to build it — which I'm already doing as part of my role."
What if the email format or BOM template changes? Medium
"The flow uses the SR number pattern from the subject line — that's been consistent. The AI Builder model is trained on our actual BOM template. If the template changes significantly, I'd need to retrain the model — that's a known maintenance task, same as updating a manual process guide. I'd flag it as a change management item."
How do we know it logged everything correctly? Easy
"During UAT I'll run it in parallel with the manual process for two weeks — every automated log gets checked against what the consultant would have written. That gives us a reconciliation baseline before we switch off manual logging."
What's the plan if it breaks in production? Easy
"The manual process doesn't go away on day one. During the first month post-go-live, consultants spot-check a sample of automated logs. If the flow fails, an alert fires and the SR gets flagged for manual processing — so there's no gap in coverage. Full rollback is just disabling the flow."

Do

  • Lead with the problem and the pain — not the technology
  • Say "30 to 77 hours a month" — the range shows you've been honest
  • Name Donovan directly when you make the ask
  • Walk the flow diagram left to right, narrate it — don't read every node
  • Say "MVP 1" — it signals discipline and makes the ask feel contained
  • Pause after your big number. Silence is fine.
  • Stop talking after your close. Don't add more.
  • If you don't know an answer, say "I'll follow up on that" — no bluffing

Don't

  • Don't open with "So today I'm going to talk about..."
  • Don't explain what Power Automate is unless asked
  • Don't read your slides word for word
  • Don't say "hopefully" or "I think it should work"
  • Don't apologise for anything upfront
  • Don't go over your time — cut the close if needed, not the ask
  • Don't skip the flow diagram — it's your proof of work
  • Don't pitch MVP 2 unless directly asked — stay in lane

If nerves hit mid-presentation

Slow down, not up. Nervous people speed up — slow is what confident sounds like. Take a breath before answering any question. You know this better than anyone in the room — you built it.
